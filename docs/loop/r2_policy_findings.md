## 1. Executive Summary & The Ranking Correction

In Round 1, the planner identified an apparent paradox in Section 5C: the p5 winner (`P2485`: 40% equity gliding to 0%, 100% floor in **Jan 2030**) was reported as securing **$234,363** in 2031 with a $0-wide range, despite its population median facility being **$192,033** and its median secured floor being **$193,041**.

### Root Cause
In `engine.conditional_2031`, the representative "median 2031 scenario" was selected by ranking `out["growth_2031"]`. For any policy that shifted 100% of its growth sleeve into floor bonds prior to 2031 (such as in Jan 2030), the growth sleeve in Jan 2031 was zero across all scenarios. Consequently, `np.argsort` sorted uniform zeros in arbitrary scenario order (picking scenario index 9,999 in a 20,000-scenario run). By random coincidence, scenario 9,999 had experienced an extreme market surge up to Jan 2030, locking in an unrepresentative 95th-percentile floor ($234.4k).

### The Fix
`engine.run` now records `out["assets_2031"]`, representing the total market wealth the client holds in January 2031:
$$\text{assets}_{2031} = \text{growth}_{2031} + \text{floor\_face} \times \text{df}(2031)$$
Whenever a policy's floor moves before 2031 (`st.floor_year < 2031`), `conditional_2031` ranks scenarios by `assets_2031`. For policies flooring in 2031 or later (`st.floor_year >= 2031`, including the IPS, S9, S8, S1, S2, and S6), ranking on `growth_2031` is retained, keeping pre-existing results byte-identical.

## 3. The Honest Objective: Two-Part Promise Score

To resolve the tension between terminal wealth optimization and co-sponsor credibility, we introduce the two-part promise objective:
$$\text{Score}(\lambda) = \text{CE}_4(\text{Facility}_{2033}) + \lambda \times \text{Median}(\text{Secured}_{2031})$$
where $\text{CE}_4$ is the certainty equivalent at CRRA risk aversion $\gamma=4$ and $\text{Secured}_{2031}$ is the floor value reaching Dec 2032.

## 4. Strategic Takeaways

1. **Why zero-floor policies win at $\lambda=0$:**
   At $\lambda = 0$, CRRA utility places zero value on what can be promised in 2031. Because equity has an expected risk premium over bonds, liquidating 60% of equities into IBTM in 2031 creates a small terminal drag of ~$2.2k in certainty equivalent.
2. **The Immediate Transition at $\lambda \ge 0.05$:**
   As soon as the client values the 2031 floor by as little as 5 cents per dollar promised ($\lambda \ge 0.05$), zero-floor policies drop precipitously. `P2361` (100% floor in Jan 2031 with 40% equity) becomes the clear leader among fully locked policies. It gives up only $1,980 in CE4 ($188,676 vs $190,656) in exchange for securing **$194,850** in Jan 2031.
3. **The Hazard of Unhedged Ladder Policies at High $\lambda$:**
   At $\lambda \ge 0.20$, policies with `lock_2027 = 0.5` (`P0053`, `P0056`) achieve higher raw sum scores because delaying the ladder hedge leaves more capital in the growth sleeve in 2027, yielding a larger floor when liquidated in 2030. However, this exposes Laura to interest-rate risk during 2027, violating her primary goal. Among fully locked policies, `P2361` is undefeated across all $\lambda > 0$.
4. **Where the IPS Stands:**
   The IPS (which uses a 60% floor rather than 100% or 0%) is an intentional compromise:
   - For $\lambda = 0$, it is within $2.2k of the best policy.
   - For $\lambda = 0.05$, it is within $4.0k of the best policy.
   - It maintains a higher median facility ($197.9k vs $188.7k for `P2361`) while still securing a credible floor of **$118k+** and compressing the co-sponsor range to under $18k.
