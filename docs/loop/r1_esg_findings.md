## 1. Executive Summary & Recommendation

### The Question
The liability-driven investment model for Laura funds ten fixed $50,000 cash flows (2033–2042) via a cash-flow-matched Treasury ladder and invests surplus cash in a growth sleeve. The economic scenario generator (`src/lgm/esg.py`) previously made two simplifications:
1. **Expected returns were treated as known:** every scenario used the same median return vector (`eq_geo`). However, the 13 capital market assumption (CMA) forecasters in `config/cma.yaml` disagree by several percentage points (US equity geometric forecasts range from 3.1% to 9.0%).
2. **Volatility was constant i.i.d. Student-t:** monthly shocks had no persistence in variance, whereas historical markets exhibit volatility clustering (e.g. 2008, 2020).

We implemented both features as opt-in extensions, calibrated them directly from published CMA dispersion and historical VTI returns, and tested six variants across 20,000 Monte Carlo paths and 184 real historical six-year backtest windows.

### Recommendation (Worker Recommendation)
> **Recommendation:** **Neither feature should be switched on for the headline numbers.** Both should remain available as opt-in sensitivity switches.
> *(Note: per the agent brief, the final decision belongs to the planning agent/team; this is our formal finding and recommendation).*

### Rationales
1. **Zero impact on payment certainty:** In every variant (`baseline`, `drift_unc_0.5x`, `drift_unc`, `drift_unc_2x`, `vol_cluster`, `both`), the funded share remains **100.0%**. Laura's ten $50,000 payments are locked into Treasuries at months 2 and 14 via cash-flow matching. Equities do not fund the liabilities, so equity drift uncertainty and volatility clustering cannot jeopardize payment certainty.
2. **Negligible impact on facility outcome:**
   - The headline facility median under `baseline` is **$197.9k**. Under `drift_unc` (1.0x calibrated covariance), it is **$197.9k** (+$6). Under `drift_unc_2x`, it is **$197.9k** (-$13).
   - Under `vol_cluster`, the median is **$197.7k** (-$219, or -0.11%). Under `both`, it is **$197.5k** (-$419, or -0.21%).
   - Tail risk ($\text{CVaR}_{5\%}$) moves from **$136.2k** (baseline) to **$136.1k** (`drift_unc`) and **$135.9k** (`both`)—a shift of barely 0.2%.
3. **History calibration worsens under drift uncertainty:**
   - In baseline, the model's 10th–90th percentile range quoted from January 2031 was already historically too wide: on re-centred history, it held **98.4%** of real outcomes (leaving only 1.1% below and 0.5% above, rather than nominal 10% tails).
   - Adding expected-return drift uncertainty expands the forecast distribution, increasing re-centred history coverage to **99.5%** (leaving **0.0%** below the 10th percentile). Expanding parameter uncertainty exacerbates the existing over-dispersion rather than resolving it.
4. **Parsimony and transparency:**
   - Adding two latent state processes adds modeling complexity without shifting any portfolio or asset-allocation decision. The IPS strategy's structural derisking (locking payments in 2027/2028 and shifting 60% of the growth sleeve into IBTM in January 2031) dominates equity distribution nuances.

## 3. Task 1: Expected-Return Uncertainty (`eq_mu_cov`)

### Calibration from `config/cma.yaml`
`calibrate_cma_dispersion()` in `src/lgm/calibrate.py` computes the sample covariance across the 13 major investment firms publishing 10-year forecasts for US, International Developed, and Emerging Markets:
- **US equity:** mean forecast 6.01%, standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{US}} = 1.48\%$
- **International Developed:** mean forecast 7.07%, standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{DEV}} = 0.72\%$
- **Emerging Markets:** mean forecast 7.58%, standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{EM}} = 1.60\%$

The $3 \times 3$ regional covariance matrix ($\times 10^4$):
$$\Sigma_{\text{CMA}} \times 10^4 = \begin{pmatrix} 2.1858 & 0.4553 & 0.5685 \\ 0.4553 & 0.5173 & 0.7462 \\ 0.5685 & 0.7462 & 2.5597 \end{pmatrix}$$

Cross-regional correlations: $\rho(\text{US}, \text{DEV}) = 0.428$, $\rho(\text{US}, \text{EM}) = 0.240$, $\rho(\text{DEV}, \text{EM}) = 0.648$.

### Asset Mapping
Assets are mapped to regions via `Group.region` from `src/lgm/growth/universe.py` (`CORE`):
- `US` (indices 0, 1, 2: VUG, VTV, VB) $\to$ US ($\text{Var} = 2.19 \times 10^{-4}$, $\text{SD} = 1.48\%$)
- `Europe`, `Japan`, `PacificXJ`, `Canada` (indices 3, 4, 5, 6: VGK, BBJP, EPP, BBCA) $\to$ intl_dev ($\text{Var} = 0.52 \times 10^{-4}$, $\text{SD} = 0.72\%$)
- `EMxChina`, `China` (indices 7, 8: EMXC, MCHI) $\to$ EM ($\text{Var} = 2.56 \times 10^{-4}$, $\text{SD} = 1.60\%$)

### Simulation Implementation & Antithetic Mirroring
For each scenario $i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$, a constant error vector $\delta_i \in \mathbb{R}^K$ is drawn once from $\mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_{\text{cov}})$ and added to that scenario's monthly drift as $\delta_i / 12.0$. For antithetic pairs, $\delta_{i + N/2} = -\delta_i$, ensuring that the cross-scenario mean drift error is identically zero.

### Nested Simulation Drift Handling (`conditional_2031`)
When re-simulating the 2031–2033 window from scenario $i$, `conditional_2031` provides an explicit argument `keep_drift: bool = True` (default):
- **Why keeping the drawn drift is Bayesian-consistent:** In Bayesian inference, parameter uncertainty represents our lack of knowledge regarding a fixed, underlying state of the world $\mu$. Once scenario $i$ has been realized under a particular drift realization $\mu_i = \mu_0 + \delta_i$, all conditional paths branching forward from that world must share that world's true parameter $\mu_i$. Redrawing new drift errors at month 48 would falsely assert that long-run expected returns randomly reroll in January 2031.
- **Implementation:** When `keep_drift=True`, scenario $i$'s drawn vector $\delta_i$ is passed via `start["eq_drift_err"]` to the inner simulation. Setting `keep_drift=False` redraws new errors, allowing both modes without breaking existing signatures.

## 4. Task 2: Volatility Clustering (`eq_garch`)

### Model Choice: Common-Factor GARCH(1,1) vs Markov Switching
We chose a **GARCH(1,1) process on the common equity factor** rather than a two-state Markov regime-switching model for five reasons:
1. **Exact unconditional variance preservation:** With variance targeting ($\omega = (1 - \alpha - \beta)$ on unit scale), $\mathbb{E}[h_m] = 1.0$ analytically for every month $m$. The unconditional variance of every equity asset is preserved identically.
2. **Deterministic volatility updates preserve antithetic pairing:** In GARCH(1,1), $h_{i, m+1}$ depends on the past squared shock $\epsilon_{i, m}^2$. Because antithetic twins satisfy $(-\epsilon)^2 = \epsilon^2$, their volatility paths are identical ($h_{i, m} = h_{i+N/2, m}$). Antithetic variance reduction is preserved with zero distortion.
3. **Zero extra random number draws:** Markov regime switching requires drawing a sequence of pseudo-random regime transitions $S_{i, m}$, consuming RNG bandwidth and potentially perturbing common random numbers across runs. GARCH updates purely from past shocks.
4. **Natural integration with Student-t shocks:** The monthly Student-t mixing variable $w_m$ provides fat tails, while GARCH provides the multi-month persistence observed in crisis periods.
5. **Continuous rather than binary states:** Financial markets exhibit a continuum of volatility levels rather than abrupt binary switches.

### Calibration on Historical VTI (2001–2026)
Calibrated on 303 monthly returns of VTI from `data/raw/yahoo` (July 2001 to September 2026) using variance targeting and maximum likelihood with Student-t innovations ($\nu = 5.0$, matching `ESGParams.t_dof`):
- $\alpha = 0.2178$ (reaction to monthly squared innovations)
- $\beta = 0.6544$ (persistence of variance)
- $\omega = 0.000251$ (unconditional variance intercept)
- **Persistence:** $\alpha + \beta = 0.8722$ (half-life of volatility shocks: $\approx 5.1$ months)
- **Unconditional annualized volatility:** $15.37\%$
- **Initial state:** Unconditional state is $h_0 = 1.0$. The filtered state at September 2026 was $h_T / \bar{h} = 0.7047$ (annualized vol $12.9\%$, reflecting the calm market in late 2026). The model uses $h_0 = 1.0$ as default to guarantee exact moment preservation over the 74-month projection.

### Invariant Checks
Across $N = 20,000$ scenarios:
- **Unconditional annual volatility:** Baseline VUG vol is $16.71\%$; GARCH vol is $16.71\%$ (diff $0.089$ percentage points, well within the required $\le 0.2$ points). Across all 9 assets, the maximum absolute difference is $0.089$ points.
- **Median 74-month return:** Maximum difference across all 9 assets is $0.0000\%/\text{yr}$ (requirement $\le 0.1\%/\text{yr}$).
- **Default off:** Setting `eq_garch=None` reproduces baseline scenarios bit-for-bit.
