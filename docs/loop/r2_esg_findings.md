## 1. Executive Summary & Recommendation

### The Question
In round 1 (`r1-esg`), testing GARCH(1,1) with a fixed unconditional initial state ($h_0 = 1.0$) lowered historical 20–80 band coverage from **81.0% to 71.7%** (re-centred) and from **75.5% to 66.3%** (as-happened). However, an unconditional start threw away the core rationale for modeling volatility clustering: in January 2031, co-sponsors observe whether recent markets have been calm or turbulent, and the quoted facility range should widen or narrow accordingly.

This round conducted the fair test:
> **Question:** When each historical path's inner simulation starts from the GARCH variance **filtered through that path's own returns up to its January 2031 month**, does the 20–80 band's coverage come back to about 80%, and does it beat the baseline conditional on the regime?

### Recommendation (Worker Recommendation)
> **Recommendation:** **Break GARCH for good. Neither the 2031 conditional quote nor the headline model should adopt GARCH (unconditional or filtered). The baseline i.i.d. Student-t model remains superior.**
> *(Note: per the agent brief, the final decision belongs to the planning agent/team; this is our formal finding and recommendation).*

### Key Reasons
1. **Coverage does NOT come back to 80%:** On re-centred history, filtered GARCH lifts 20–80 coverage by only 1.7 percentage points (from 71.7% to **73.4%**), remaining **7.6 percentage points worse than the baseline's 81.0%**. On as-happened history, filtered GARCH achieves **69.0%**, still **6.5 percentage points below the baseline's 75.5%**.
2. **Excessive downside misses in normal markets:** While filtered GARCH widens appropriately during crises, in normal markets ($h \approx 0.70$–$1.15$) it narrows the band ($w_{20} = \$14.9\text{k}$ vs $\$17.0\text{k}$ baseline), causing **21.3%** of real re-centred outcomes to land below the quoted 20th percentile (violating the nominal 20% downside bound).
3. **The 24-month horizon washes out volatility memory:** GARCH persistence on monthly data is $\alpha + \beta = 0.8722$, giving a half-life of $\approx 5.1$ months. Over the 24-month window (Jan 2031 to Jan 2033), $(0.8722)^{24} = 0.035$—meaning **over 96% of any initial variance shock has evaporated** by settlement.
4. **The 2031 IBTM floor move mutes equity risk:** In January 2031, Laura's IPS moves **60% of the growth sleeve into short/intermediate Treasuries (IBTM)**. Equity exposure is cut by 60% precisely during the 2031–2033 window, sharply suppressing the portfolio impact of equity volatility dynamics.
5. **Zero benefit to decision-making:** Payment certainty remains 100.0% in all cases, and the median facility changes by less than 0.12% ($-\$234$).

## 2. Task 1: The Variance Filter (`filter_equity_garch`)

We added `filter_equity_garch()` to `src/lgm/calibrate.py`:
$$h_{t+1} = (1 - \alpha - \beta) + \alpha \frac{(r_t - \mu)^2}{\bar{\sigma}^2} + \beta h_t$$
where $\alpha = 0.2178, \beta = 0.6544$, $\bar{\sigma}^2 = \text{Var}(r - \mu)$, normalized so that unconditional variance multiplier $\mathbb{E}[h] = 1.0$.

### Verification & Invariants
- **Exact recovery of simulated paths:** Filtering the generator's own simulated returns recovers its simulated `garch_h` paths with **$0.0$ maximum absolute error** across all scenarios and months (verified in `tests/test_esg_garch_filter.py`).
- **Recovery of today's state:** Filtering monthly VTI from July 2001 to September 2026 yields $h_{\text{today}} = 0.7047$, matching the **0.70** reported in round 1 (`r1_esg_findings.md`).

## 4. Regime Analysis: Why Does Filtered GARCH Behave This Way?

### 1. In Calm Markets, Filtered GARCH Narrows Bands Safely
In the calm tercile, mean filtered variance is $h = 0.559$. Filtered GARCH narrows the quoted 20–80 band by **-$3,011** (re-centred width drops from $\$17,594$ to $\$14,583$). Coverage drops from 96.8% to 88.7%, closer to the nominal target, with only 6.5% falling below the 20th percentile.

### 2. In Turbulent Markets, Filtered GARCH Prevents Crisis Under-Coverage
In the turbulent tercile, mean filtered variance is $h = 1.859$. Unconditional GARCH fails badly here, quoting narrow bands that cover only 50.8% of as-happened paths. Filtered GARCH corrects this by expanding the 10–90 width by **+$3,843** over unconditional GARCH (to $\$29,493$), restoring coverage to 65.6% (beating baseline's 60.7%).

### 3. The Fatal Flaw: Normal Markets Fail Under GARCH
In the normal tercile ($h \approx 0.895$), filtered GARCH tightens the band slightly ($w_{20} = \$14,896$ vs $\$16,991$). Because the unconditional Student-t baseline has fat tails, narrowing the band in average markets exposes the quote to moderate negative shocks:
- **21.3%** of real re-centred paths land below the 20th percentile under filtered GARCH (vs only 14.8% in baseline).
- On as-happened data, below-20 misses triple from **3.3%** in baseline to **9.8%** in filtered GARCH.
Because real markets spend most of their time in the normal regime, this flaw drags down overall coverage across the full sample.

## 6. Statistical Limitation: Overlapping History

The 184 historical paths span May 2005 to September 2026 (257 months). Because each path is 74 months long, the sample contains only about:
$$N_{\text{eff}} \approx \frac{257}{74} \approx 3.5 \text{ to } 4 \text{ independent 6-year economic cycles.}$$

- The **turbulent tercile** is dominated by paths touching the 2008–2009 Global Financial Crisis and March 2020 COVID shock.
- The **calm tercile** is dominated by the mid-2010s quantitative easing bull market.
- Because paths overlap heavily, coverage statistics have wider sampling uncertainty than independent trials. However, the qualitative finding—that a 24-month horizon with a 60% Treasury derisking step wipes out the value of a short-memory volatility process—is driven by structural portfolio mechanics, not sample noise.
